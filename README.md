# CheshireCat

I've been thinking about starting a short-form video channel on YouTube, Instagram, and TikTok, mostly about network science and complex systems. Think 3Blue1Brown, Vsauce, Vox, etc. Short, visually interesting videos that start with a simple question and lead into some cool math, science, or a surprising real-world phenomenon.

After making the animations for my [CCS 2026 talk](https://thechesirecat.github.io/ccs2026-talk/) using Reveal.js and Claude, I've become a lot more confident that this is something I could sustain. Ideally, I'd like to get to a point where I can put out a video every week or so without it taking over my life.

I've put together an initial [proposal](https://thechesirecat.github.io/ChesireCat/) with some ideas for the format, visual style, and a possible first season.

The episode breakdown is mostly drawn from Barabási's Network Science textbook for now, since I was initially thinking of using this as a way to work through material for my quals. But I don't want to restrict it to textbook material, or even network science. There's so much interesting work happening in complex systems, including within our own group and institute, that I think we could find plenty of good stories to tell.

I also think there's an opportunity here. There's been a noticeable amount of AI-generated math and science content on Instagram lately, often using Manim-style animations and AI narration. Some of it is genuinely quite good, but a lot of it looks and sounds very similar. I think there's room for something with more personality, good storytelling, interesting visualizations, and an actual human voice.

If this works out, I could also see it becoming a nice outreach channel for the group or institute, potentially getting more people interested in our research, network science, and maybe even our graduate programs. I'm happy for it to have a university affiliation if it gets somewhere.

For now, I'll handle the animations, narration, and production. What I'd really appreciate from others is **ideas and feedback**. These could be interesting results from your own research, papers you've come across, questions you've always found fascinating, or phenomena that you think would make for a good visual explanation.

The first video I'm working on is about the **friendship paradox**: why your friends tend to have more friends than you do, and how that seemingly trivial observation has some surprisingly useful consequences.

I'll share a draft once I have something worth showing.

Thanks for coming,
